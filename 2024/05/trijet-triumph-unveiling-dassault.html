Trijet Triumph: Unveiling the Dassault Falcon 50's Third Engine Innovation

Falcon50, DassaultAviation, TrijetDesign, SupercriticalWing, BusinessJets, AerospaceEngineering, AviationHistory, ExtendedRangeJets, isaacupdate, sseg

SSEGUPost2024-05-05

Dassault Aviation, with almost a century of aviation excellence, has not only shaped Europe's military defense but also soared in the business jet domain. Among its fleet, the Falcon series stands tall, spanning from the nimble Falcon 10 to the forthcoming Falcon 10X, promising unparalleled ultra-long-range travel.



At the heart of this lineage lies the Falcon 50, a pioneer in its own right. From the 1970s until its curtain call in 2008, it captivated business jet connoisseurs worldwide. Yet, its significance transcends popularity; it marked Dassault's foray into trijet design and introduced the supercritical wing, revolutionizing aerospace engineering. Let's delve into the saga of this iconic aircraft and unravel its enduring allure.


The Genesis:

Conceived amidst the 1970s' demand for extended-range business jets, Dassault embarked on the Falcon 50's journey in 1974. The vision? A jet capable of spanning 3,400 nautical miles, bridging continents nonstop. Just two swift years later, in November 1976, the maiden Falcon 50 prototype took flight. Midway through development, Dassault debuted revolutionary supercritical wings, elevating aerodynamic efficiency. By May 1977, the upgraded prototype graced the skies, heralding a new era in aviation design.


Into the Blue:

Certification swiftly followed, with the French government's nod in February 1979 and the FAA's endorsement the same year. Production commenced, with Aerospatiale contributing fuselage construction, while Dassault crafted wings and undertook final assembly. Over 250 Falcon 50s took wing, becoming fixtures in the skies.


Evolution in Flight:

In 1995, Dassault unveiled the Falcon 50EX, a testament to relentless innovation. Enhanced speed, heightened ceilings, and extended ranges characterized this iteration. Avionics received a modern makeover, complementing upgraded engines. Over a decade, 100 Falcon 50EXs joined the ranks, enriching Dassault's legacy.


Beyond Boundaries:

Recognizing emerging needs, Dassault reimagined the Falcon 50 for maritime and surveillance roles, birthing the Guardian 50 variant. The French Navy embraced this adaptation, deploying it for diverse missions, from environmental monitoring to maritime surveillance.


Global Influence:

The Falcon 50's versatility resonated globally, finding a place in numerous government fleets worldwide. From ferrying dignitaries to nurturing future aviators, its impact spanned continents. Notable operators included the air forces of Bolivia, Bulgaria, Djibouti, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Italy, Jordan, Portugal, Serbia, Spain, Sudan, and Switzerland.


Engineering Marvels:

Central to the Falcon 50's prowess was its trijet configuration, powered initially by Garrett TFE 731-3 turbofans and later by Honeywell TFE 731–40 engines in the Falcon 50EX. These engines, coupled with the innovative S-duct air intake, delivered formidable thrusts. Supercritical wings not only enhanced performance but also birthed a legacy, shaping subsequent Dassault aircraft.



Specifications Overview:

From its sleek dimensions to its skyward aspirations, the Falcon 50 and Falcon 50EX epitomized excellence. With impressive cruising speeds, extended ranges, and soaring ceilings, they redefined the super-midsize class, accommodating up to nine passengers in opulent comfort.


Legacy in Flight:

The Falcon 50's legacy endures, transcending its production years. Its trijet innovation and supercritical wing design paved the way for future aviation marvels. As Dassault continues to push boundaries, the Falcon 50 remains an indelible chapter in its storied history, a testament to engineering brilliance, and a harbinger of aviation's future.


In summary, the Falcon 50's journey from concept to skyward triumph epitomizes Dassault's commitment to excellence, heralding a new era in business aviation.
