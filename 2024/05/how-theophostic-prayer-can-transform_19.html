Theophostic Prayer Ministry (TPM) is a unique approach to spiritual counseling. It combines prayer and divine guidance to facilitate emotional healing.


Founded by Dr. Ed Smith, TPM has gained global recognition. It's used by individuals seeking spiritual growth and healing, as well as by counselors and religious leaders.

The term "Theophostic" is a combination of "Theo" (God) and "phostic" (light). This reflects the ministry's focus on illuminating truth and dispelling lies that cause emotional pain.

TPM is not just another form of prayer ministry. It's a distinct practice with its own principles and processes, deeply rooted in Christian beliefs.

This article aims to provide an in-depth understanding of TPM. We'll explore its origins, principles, and practices, and discuss its potential benefits and controversies.

Whether you're a spiritual seeker, a counselor, or simply curious about alternative prayer ministries, this article is for you. Join us as we delve into the transformative power of Theophostic Prayer Ministry.

Understanding Theophostic Prayer Ministry

Theophostic Prayer Ministry (TPM) is a faith-based approach to emotional healing. It's grounded in the belief that God, as the ultimate source of truth, can bring light into the darkest corners of our minds.

TPM is not a quick fix or a magic bullet. It's a process that requires commitment, openness, and a willingness to confront painful memories. The goal is not to erase these memories, but to revisit them in a safe space, under the guidance of a trained facilitator.

The facilitator's role is not to provide answers or solutions. Instead, they guide the participant through the process, allowing the Holy Spirit to reveal the truth. This truth, according to TPM, is the key to emotional healing.

TPM is not just about addressing symptoms of emotional distress. It aims to identify and address the root causes of this distress, often found in past experiences and false beliefs.

While TPM is deeply rooted in Christian beliefs, it's not limited to Christians. It's been adopted by individuals and communities across various cultures and countries, attesting to its universal appeal.

The Origins and Founder of TPM

Theophostic Prayer Ministry was founded by Dr. Ed Smith in the 1990s. Dr. Smith, a pastor and counselor, developed TPM as a response to the limitations he saw in traditional therapy.

His goal was to create a method that went beyond managing symptoms. He wanted to address the root causes of emotional pain, which he believed were often rooted in false beliefs or "lies" that individuals held about themselves.

Dr. Smith's work has been influential in the field of Christian counseling. His approach has been adopted by many practitioners worldwide, contributing to the growth and recognition of TPM.

The Principles of Theophostic Prayer

Theophostic Prayer Ministry is based on several key principles. The first is the belief in the healing power of truth. According to TPM, emotional pain is often caused by "lie-based thinking," or false beliefs about oneself, others, or God.

The second principle is the role of the Holy Spirit in revealing this truth. In TPM, the facilitator does not provide the truth. Instead, they guide the participant in inviting the Holy Spirit to illuminate the truth.

The third principle is the importance of addressing the root causes of emotional pain. TPM is not about managing symptoms. It's about identifying and addressing the underlying issues that cause these symptoms.

The fourth principle is the integration of prayer and scripture. TPM is deeply rooted in Christian beliefs and practices, and prayer is a central component of the process.

Finally, TPM emphasizes the importance of trained facilitators. These facilitators guide the process, but they do not dictate the outcomes. The healing, according to TPM, comes from God.

Theophostic Prayer vs. Traditional Therapy

While there are similarities between TPM and traditional therapy, there are also key differences. Traditional therapy often focuses on managing symptoms and improving coping skills. TPM, on the other hand, aims to address the root causes of emotional distress.

In traditional therapy, the therapist often plays an active role in providing insights and solutions. In TPM, the facilitator's role is more passive. They guide the process, but the revelation of truth is believed to come from the Holy Spirit.

Another key difference is the integration of faith in TPM. While some forms of therapy may incorporate spiritual elements, TPM is deeply rooted in Christian beliefs and practices.

Despite these differences, TPM is not intended to replace traditional therapy. Instead, it can be a valuable complement, offering a spiritual perspective to the healing process.

The Process of Theophostic Prayer Ministry

Theophostic Prayer Ministry is a structured process. It begins with the identification of emotional pain. This pain is often linked to a specific memory or event from the participant's past.

The facilitator guides the participant in revisiting this memory. This is not about reliving the pain, but about viewing it from a new perspective. The participant is encouraged to invite the Holy Spirit into this memory, asking for truth and guidance.

The next step is the revelation of truth. This is believed to come from the Holy Spirit, not the facilitator. The truth, according to TPM, is the key to emotional healing.

The process concludes with a time of reflection and prayer. The participant is encouraged to meditate on the revealed truth, allowing it to replace the lies that caused the emotional pain.

The facilitator then guides the participant in identifying a specific memory or event linked to their emotional pain. This is done in a safe and supportive environment, with the facilitator providing guidance and support throughout the process.

The session concludes with a time of reflection and prayer. The participant is encouraged to meditate on the revealed truth, allowing it to replace the lies that caused the emotional pain.

The goal of TPM is not to erase these memories, but to revisit them in the light of truth. This truth, according to TPM, comes from the Holy Spirit. The facilitator's role is to guide the participant in inviting the Holy Spirit into these memories.

The revelation of truth is a key moment in the TPM process. It's believed to bring healing and freedom, replacing the lies that caused the emotional pain.

Addressing "Lie-Based Pain"

"Lie-based pain" is a central concept in Theophostic Prayer Ministry. It refers to the emotional pain caused by false beliefs or "lies" that we hold about ourselves, others, or God.

These lies often stem from past experiences and memories. They can shape our perceptions and responses, causing ongoing emotional distress.

The goal of TPM is to replace these lies with truth. This truth, according to TPM, comes from the Holy Spirit. It's believed to bring healing and freedom, transforming the way we view ourselves and our past.

The Controversy and Criticism of TPM

Despite its reported benefits, Theophostic Prayer Ministry has faced controversy and criticism. Some mental health professionals express concern about the potential for retraumatization in revisiting painful memories. They argue that this process should be guided by trained therapists, not lay counselors.

Others question the theological basis of TPM. They argue that it deviates from traditional Christian teachings, placing too much emphasis on personal revelation and not enough on scripture. They express concern about the potential for manipulation or misuse in the hands of untrained facilitators.

Theophostic Prayer Ministry has responded to these critiques by emphasizing the importance of training and certification for facilitators. They also stress the role of the Holy Spirit in revealing truth, not the facilitator. They argue that TPM is a tool for healing, not a replacement for traditional therapy or biblical teaching.
