Farm and Agricultural Jobs With Visa Sponsorship

This guide covers which countries hire foreign farm workers, what jobs qualify, requirements, salary, how visa sponsorship works, and how to apply so

SSEGUPost2026-08-30

 

Farms around the world are hiring in 2026. Due to seasonal demand, aging local workforces, and labor shortages, many countries are actively recruiting foreign workers for farm and agricultural jobs and offering visa sponsorship.

If you have experience in planting, harvesting, livestock, dairy, or greenhouse work, you can legally work abroad with stable pay, accommodation, and a clear path to extend your stay. From fruit picking in Canada to dairy farming in Australia and vineyard work in Europe, employers are sponsoring visas to keep food production running.

This guide covers which countries hire foreign farm workers, what jobs qualify, requirements, salary, how visa sponsorship works, and how to apply so you get hired faster.

Why Farms Offer Visa Sponsorship

Agriculture is seasonal and labor intensive. Crops need to be planted, tended, and harvested at specific times. Local workers often prefer year-round jobs in cities.

At the same time, global food demand is rising. To avoid crop loss, farms and agribusinesses sponsor foreign workers through government programs. Visa sponsorship means the employer offers you a job and supports your work visa application. Many also provide housing, meals, transport, and help with paperwork.

For you, it means legal work, fair wages, and experience that can lead to longer contracts or permanent residency in some countries.

Top Countries Hiring Farm Workers With Visa Sponsorship

Several countries have active agricultural recruitment programs in 2026.

1. Canada

Canada’s Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program and Temporary Foreign Worker Program bring in thousands of workers each year. Jobs include fruit and vegetable picking, greenhouse work, dairy, and meat processing. Contracts are usually 8 months. Many employers provide free housing.

2. United States

The US H-2A visa is for seasonal agricultural workers. Farms hire internationally for planting, harvesting, and livestock. Common crops are apples, berries, vegetables, and tobacco. Housing and transport are required by law to be provided by the employer.

3. Australia

Australia’s Agricultural Visa and Pacific Australia Labour Mobility scheme sponsor workers for farms, orchards, and meat processing. High demand in Queensland, Victoria, and Western Australia. Work can be seasonal or up to 4 years.

4. United Kingdom

The UK Seasonal Worker visa allows farms to hire foreign workers for 6 months. Main jobs are fruit picking, packing, and horticulture. Many farms also sponsor skilled workers for dairy and farm management roles.

5. New Zealand

New Zealand’s Recognised Seasonal Employer scheme hires workers for vineyards, orchards, and dairy. Contracts are 7 months. Employers must provide accommodation and meet strict labor standards.

6. European Union - Germany, Spain, Italy, France

EU farms hire seasonal workers for grapes, olives, tomatoes, and flowers. Germany and Spain have bilateral agreements with other countries. Many jobs are 3 to 9 months with housing included.

In-Demand Farm and Agricultural Jobs That Get Visa Sponsorship

Not every farm job qualifies. Employers sponsor roles with labor shortages.

Most sponsored roles

1. Fruit and Vegetable Pickers: Berries, apples, grapes, tomatoes

2. Greenhouse Workers: Planting, pruning, and packing in controlled environments

3. Dairy Farm Workers: Milking, feeding, and animal care

4. Livestock Workers: Cattle, sheep, pigs

5. Farm Equipment Operators: Tractors, harvesters

6. Packing and Processing Staff: Sorting and packaging produce

7. Vineyard and Orchard Workers: Pruning, thinning, harvesting

8. Farm Supervisors and Managers: For workers with experience and leadership skills

Experience helps, but many entry-level jobs train you on site. Physical fitness and reliability are most important.

Requirements to Qualify for Sponsored Agricultural Jobs

Requirements are simpler than for tech or office jobs, but still important.

1. Age: Usually 18 to 45 years old, though some programs accept up to 55

2. Physical fitness: Ability to do manual labor outdoors in all weather

3. Experience: 6 months to 2 years in farming is preferred but not always required

4. Basic language: Basic English for Canada, US, UK, Australia, NZ. Some EU farms accept other languages

5. Health and background check: Standard for all work visas

6. Willingness to relocate to rural areas and work shifts

If you have skills in machinery, animal health, or irrigation, mention it. It increases your chances and pay.

How Farm Visa Sponsorship Works Step by Step

The process is straightforward and often faster than other industries.

Step 1: Find an employer who sponsors

Look for farms, labor contractors, and government job portals that list "visa sponsorship available." Many countries have official agricultural job boards.

Step 2: Apply and interview

Send a simple CV with work history, physical ability, and availability. Interviews are often short and on video or phone.

Step 3: Job offer and sponsorship

If selected, you get a contract. The employer applies for labor approval and sends you documents for your visa.

Step 4: Visa application

You apply at the embassy or online. Processing takes 2 weeks to 2 months depending on the country and season.

Step 5: Travel and start work

Many employers arrange airport pickup and provide housing on the farm. Training is usually 1 to 3 days on site.

Benefits of Farm Jobs With Visa Sponsorship

Working on a farm abroad offers more than income.

1. Legal work status with labor law protections

2. Free or subsidized housing on or near the farm

3. No degree required for most roles

4. Fast hiring: Many farms hire within weeks

5. Return worker programs: Good workers are invited back every season

6. Path to longer visas: Australia and Canada offer ways to extend or transition to other visas

Challenges to Know Before You Apply

Farm work is rewarding but physically demanding.

Hard physical work: Long hours standing, bending, and lifting

Rural locations: Farms are often far from cities and shopping

Seasonal work: Contracts end after harvest. You may need to find another job

Weather: You will work in heat, rain, and cold

Teamwork: You will live and work closely with other workers

Workers who succeed are hardworking, punctual, and follow safety rules. Farms value reliability above all.

Where to Find Legitimate Farm Jobs With Visa Sponsorship

Avoid agents who charge large fees. Real farm employers do not ask you to pay for a job.

Trusted sources

1. Government portals: Canada Job Bank, USA SeasonalJobs.dol.gov, Seek Australia

2. Official programs: SAWP, H-2A, RSE, PALM

3. Licensed recruitment agencies specializing in agriculture

4. Farm and grower association websites

5. Embassy websites of countries with bilateral agreements

Red flags: requests for payment to process visa, guaranteed visa without interview, or jobs that ask for passport upfront.

How to Make Your Application Stand Out

Even with high demand, good applications get picked first.

1. Keep your CV simple. List farm experience, crops worked with, and physical ability

2. State your availability. Be clear about which months you can work

3. Highlight reliability. Mention if you finished previous contracts

4. Show flexibility. Willingness to do different tasks helps

5. Apply early. Most hiring happens 2 to 3 months before planting or harvest season

6. Have documents ready: Passport, medical certificate, and references

FAQ: Farm and Agricultural Jobs With Visa Sponsorship

1. Do I need farming experience to get sponsored?

No. Many entry-level jobs train you. Experience with manual labor or living in rural areas helps.

2. Who pays for visa, flights, and housing?

It depends on the program. US H-2A and Canadian SAWP require employers to pay transport and provide housing. Other countries vary. Always read the contract.

3. Can I bring my family?

Most seasonal farm visas do not allow dependents. Skilled agricultural visas in Canada and Australia may allow family.

4. How long are contracts?

Usually 3 to 9 months. Some dairy and livestock jobs are year-round.

5. Is English required?

Basic English helps for safety and instructions. Some programs accept other languages and provide translators.

6. Can I extend my visa or return next year?

Yes. Many workers return to the same farm for multiple seasons. Good performance increases your chances.

Tips for Success on Your First Farm Job Abroad

Getting hired is step one. Doing well is step two.

1. Follow safety rules. Use gloves, boots, and protective gear

2. Be on time every day. Farms run on strict schedules

3. Work as a team. Help coworkers and communicate

4. Take care of your health. Drink water, rest, and eat well

5. Ask questions. Learn new skills like machinery or animal care

6. Keep records. Save pay stubs and contract for future applications

Farm and agricultural jobs with visa sponsorship are one of the most accessible ways to work abroad in 2026. The work is honest, the demand is real, and employers need you.

If you are hardworking and ready for physical work, this is your chance to earn money, gain international experience, and possibly return season after season.
