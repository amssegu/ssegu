Best Jobs for Fresh Graduates in Uganda and Where to Find Them

Looking for jobs as a fresh graduate in Uganda? these are some entry-level jobs, graduate trainee programmes, salaries, requirements, job sites

SSEGUPost2026-09-20

  



Graduating from university is an achievement, but finding your first job in Uganda can be a different sort of challenge. Many entry-level vacancies ask for experience, while employers offering graduate positions may receive hundreds of applications.

The good news is that you do not have to wait for the perfect job to begin your career.

The best first job is usually one that gives you a useful combination of income, practical experience, professional contacts and skills you can take to your next position.

For fresh graduates in Uganda, opportunities can be found in graduate trainee programmes, administration, sales, finance, IT, customer service, research, NGOs, education, engineering and other entry-level roles.

What are the best jobs for fresh graduates in Uganda?

There is no single "best" job for every graduate. A good choice depends on your degree, existing skills, location and long-term career plans.

However, the following are some of the most useful entry points for recent graduates.

The important point is that a graduate job does not necessarily have to carry the exact title associated with your degree.

A computer science graduate, for example, might begin in IT support, systems administration, data operations or software development. A business graduate could enter sales, finance, procurement, administration or operations.

1. Graduate trainee programmes

Graduate trainee programmes are among the best opportunities to watch if you have recently completed university.

Unlike an ordinary vacancy that may expect previous employment, a genuine graduate programme is normally designed to develop people who are early in their careers.

Recent examples in Uganda show how varied these programmes can be.

In 2026, Uganda Registration Services Bureau advertised ICT graduate trainee positions for graduates in fields including Computer Science, Information Technology, Software Engineering, Information Systems, Cyber Security and Data Science. The one-year programme included practical exposure to areas such as IT support, networking, software development, DevOps, cloud infrastructure and data security.

Other 2026 graduate opportunities included programmes in finance, engineering, sales and marketing. TotalEnergies Marketing Uganda, for example, advertised a Young Graduate Programme targeting recent graduates, particularly in engineering, finance, sales and marketing.

Graduate programmes can be competitive, and eligibility may be quite specific. Some employers limit applications to people who graduated within the previous two years or set age, academic-grade or subject requirements.

Before applying, check:

  • Graduation year requirement

  • Degree and academic requirements

  • Age limits, if any

  • Whether the programme is paid

  • Duration

  • Location and working hours

  • Whether there is a possibility of employment afterwards

Do not assume that a graduate trainee programme guarantees a permanent job. Some explicitly state that successful completion does not guarantee employment.

2. Administration and office jobs

Administrative positions are worth considering if you have good computer, communication and organisational skills.

Typical entry-level responsibilities can include:

  • Preparing documents and reports

  • Data entry and record keeping

  • Scheduling meetings

  • Handling emails and correspondence

  • Maintaining databases

  • Supporting procurement or accounts departments

  • Customer reception and communication

  • Filing and document management

These jobs may not sound glamorous, but they can provide valuable experience with the systems and routines used in professional workplaces.

A recent Kampala vacancy for a fresh graduate data-entry administrator, for example, involved collecting, verifying, organising and entering information into digital systems.

If you start in administration, try to build transferable skills rather than remaining limited to repetitive tasks. Excel, reporting, bookkeeping, data analysis, CRM systems and project coordination can make your next application considerably stronger.

3. Sales and business development

Sales is one of the more accessible career paths for graduates because employers can often train people who have strong communication and interpersonal skills.

Entry-level positions may include:

  • Sales representative

  • Business development assistant

  • Account executive

  • Relationship officer

  • Field sales officer

  • Customer acquisition officer

  • Marketing assistant

Sales can also be a useful career foundation because you learn how customers make decisions, how businesses generate revenue and how to communicate value.

However, read sales adverts carefully.

Some jobs offer a fixed salary plus commission. Others rely heavily on commission or incentives. Before accepting an offer, ask how much of the compensation is guaranteed and how targets are calculated.

A job that advertises "up to UGX 2 million per month" may not mean that you will receive UGX 2 million every month.

4. Finance and accounting jobs

Accounting and finance graduates can look beyond traditional "accountant" vacancies.

Useful entry-level search terms include:

  • Accounts assistant

  • Finance assistant

  • Accounts trainee

  • Audit assistant

  • Credit officer

  • Banking trainee

  • Finance intern

  • Payroll assistant

  • Reconciliation assistant

Recent Ugandan graduate recruitment illustrates the range. In 2026, UGAFODE advertised a graduate trainee programme for recent graduates interested in building careers in banking, while another Kampala graduate trainee vacancy advertised a finance role with a listed salary of UGX 300,000 to UGX 500,000.

That salary should not be treated as the standard salary for all graduate jobs. Pay varies considerably by employer, industry, qualifications, experience and role.

When comparing offers, consider the entire package rather than looking only at the headline salary.

5. IT, software and data jobs

Technology remains an attractive path for graduates who can demonstrate practical ability.

Possible entry-level roles include:

  • IT support technician

  • Junior software developer

  • Systems support assistant

  • Data entry or data operations assistant

  • Junior data analyst

  • Network support technician

  • QA/testing assistant

  • Cybersecurity trainee

  • Technical support representative

The major advantage in technology is that a portfolio can provide evidence of ability before you have a long employment history.

For example, a computer science graduate could show:

  • A small web application

  • A GitHub project

  • A database project

  • An automation script

  • A mobile application

  • A data-analysis project

  • A website built for a real or simulated business

This is increasingly relevant to recruitment. Uganda Revenue Authority describes its "experiential hiring" approach as focusing on demonstrated skills and abilities alongside traditional qualifications.

That does not mean your degree has become irrelevant. It means you should give employers something concrete to evaluate.

6. Research, NGO and project work

Graduates in social sciences, development studies, public health, agriculture, statistics and related disciplines can look for research and programme positions.

Common entry-level titles include:

  • Research assistant

  • Field officer

  • Data collector

  • Programme assistant

  • Project assistant

  • Monitoring and evaluation assistant

  • Community mobilisation officer

  • Enumerator

These positions can involve travel and fieldwork, particularly outside Kampala.

Before accepting one, check:

  • Duty station

  • Transport arrangements

  • Accommodation

  • Contract duration

  • Working hours

  • Field allowances

  • Payment schedule

  • Whether the position is temporary or permanent

Short-term project work is not necessarily a bad job. A six-month position where you learn data collection, reporting, monitoring and project implementation can be more valuable than spending the same six months applying randomly to unrelated jobs.

7. Teaching and tutoring

Teaching remains another possible route, particularly for graduates with education qualifications or strong subject knowledge.

You can consider:

  • Primary or secondary teaching

  • Private tutoring

  • Academic support

  • Online tutoring

  • Teaching assistant positions

  • Examination preparation

Requirements vary between schools and roles, so do not assume that holding a degree automatically qualifies you for every teaching position.

Private tutoring can also be used as a side income while searching for permanent employment, particularly if you have strong knowledge of mathematics, sciences, English, ICT or other high-demand subjects.

8. Engineering and technical graduate jobs

Engineering graduates should search for graduate engineer, trainee engineer, technical assistant and junior engineering positions rather than waiting for a vacancy with the exact title of their degree.

Recent Ugandan recruitment has included graduate engineering opportunities. For example, Uganda National Oil Company advertised a Graduate Electrical and Instrumentation Engineer position in 2026. The advert required applicants to use UNOC's recruitment system and stated that the successful applicant had to be willing to work at any UNOC office location.

This is a useful reminder that graduate opportunities may come with conditions involving location, mobility and working environment.

Read the full job description before applying rather than relying on the job title.

How much do fresh graduates earn in Uganda?

There is no reliable single salary figure for fresh graduates in Uganda.

Entry-level pay varies substantially between industries and employers. Internships, graduate trainee programmes, NGOs, banks, technology companies, SMEs and government positions can have very different compensation structures.

Some current job adverts publish salaries, while many do not. For example, one 2026 graduate trainee finance vacancy listed UGX 300,000 to UGX 500,000, while other graduate programmes disclosed no salary in the public advert.

For that reason, be cautious with articles claiming that "the average graduate salary in Uganda is exactly X."

Instead, compare the actual offer:

Monthly pay + allowances + benefits + working hours + transport costs + accommodation + career value

A UGX 700,000 job close to home may leave you better off than a UGX 900,000 job that requires expensive daily transport.

For government positions, use the current Ministry of Public Service salary structures rather than relying on old salary tables circulating on social media. The Ministry publishes the salary structure for the relevant financial year and other public-service guidance.

Where can fresh graduates find jobs in Uganda?

Do not depend on a single job website.

Use several channels and check the employer's own website whenever possible.

1. BrighterMonday Uganda

BrighterMonday Uganda jobs

BrighterMonday currently provides filters for no-experience, entry-level and internship/graduate opportunities. It also allows job seekers to filter by location and job function.

2. Great Uganda Jobs

Great Uganda Jobs

It is useful for discovering vacancies, but when you find a position there, verify the employer and application instructions against the organisation's official website where possible.

3. Employer career pages

Large employers and public institutions frequently advertise directly through their own websites.

For example, URSB publishes its vacancies and application procedures on its careers page. Its current recruitment process uses an online application system and requires applicants to provide a National Identification Number.

Similarly, Uganda Revenue Authority's careers information provides information about its recruitment processes, including experiential hiring.

4. Public-sector recruitment

Government jobs should be checked through official public-service recruitment channels rather than forwarded WhatsApp messages alone.

The Ministry of Public Service provides official information on public-service recruitment, structures and terms, while the Public Service Commission and District Service Commissions are involved in public recruitment. The Ministry has also highlighted the use of digital e-recruitment systems to improve transparency.

5. LinkedIn and professional networks

LinkedIn can be useful for graduate roles, particularly in technology, finance, consulting, NGOs and multinational organisations.

But do not simply click "Easy Apply" all day.

Follow companies you genuinely want to work for, connect with people in your field and monitor their career pages.

How to get a job with no experience

The classic graduate problem is simple:

"How am I supposed to get experience when every employer wants experience?"

The answer is to build evidence of ability outside formal employment.

If you have no previous job, your CV can still contain:

  • University projects

  • Internships

  • Volunteer work

  • Student leadership

  • Freelance work

  • Personal projects

  • Research

  • Relevant certifications

  • Community activities

  • Portfolio work

Do not write:

Hardworking graduate with excellent computer skills.

Show the evidence instead:

Built a small inventory management website using HTML, CSS, JavaScript and MySQL as a university project.

The second statement gives an employer something concrete.

Build a small portfolio

You do not need a sophisticated website.

A Google Drive folder, GitHub profile, PDF portfolio or simple personal website can be enough, depending on the profession.

Aim for two or three relevant examples.

A marketing graduate could show a campaign plan.

A graphic designer could show selected designs.

An IT graduate could show working software.

An accounting graduate could show a properly constructed financial model using fictional data.

A research graduate could demonstrate data cleaning and analysis using a sample dataset.

Remove confidential information from any work you did for an employer or organisation.

How to avoid fake graduate jobs and recruitment scams

Fresh graduates are particularly vulnerable to recruitment scams because they are often desperate for their first opportunity.

Be suspicious if someone asks you to pay money to:

  • Secure a job

  • Guarantee an interview

  • Be shortlisted

  • Buy recruitment forms

  • Pay a "processing fee"

  • Pay for a compulsory training package

  • Pay someone to influence the recruitment panel

A legitimate employer may charge for something unrelated to recruitment in certain circumstances, but a demand for money in exchange for employment should be treated as a major warning sign.

Official employers explicitly warn applicants about this. URA states that it does not charge money for recruitment, while World Vision Uganda's 2026 graduate internship advert similarly stated that it does not charge for recruitment, interviews or medical checks.

Also verify:

  1. The organisation's official website.

  2. The company's physical and contact information.

  3. Whether the vacancy appears on the employer's own careers page.

  4. The sender's email address.

  5. Whether the application process matches the official advert.

  6. Whether the job description makes sense for the organisation.

Do not send your National ID, academic documents or financial information to an unverified recruiter simply because the advert looks professional.

How to prepare for your first graduate job

Before applying seriously, prepare these five things.

1. A targeted CV

Keep the CV easy to scan and adjust it for the role.

Put your most relevant education, skills, projects and experience where the recruiter will see them quickly.

2. A simple cover letter

Do not write three pages about your childhood ambition.

Explain why you are interested in the role, what relevant skills you have and what you can contribute.

3. Academic documents

Keep clear copies of your degree certificate, transcripts and other relevant qualifications.

Some employers may request additional academic documents during the recruitment process.

4. A professional email address

Your email address should ideally use your name rather than an old nickname or gaming username.

5. Interview preparation

Research the organisation before the interview.

Know what it does, who its customers are, what the advertised role involves and why you applied.

Prepare examples showing how you solved a problem, worked with other people, handled responsibility or learned something difficult.

Should you accept a low-paying first job?

Sometimes.

But "any job is better than no job" is not always good advice.

A first job can make sense if the pay is modest but you are gaining meaningful experience, professional contacts, training or a recognised skill.

It becomes much less attractive when the employer expects excessive hours, provides no learning opportunity, keeps changing the agreed pay or asks you to pay money to work.

Before accepting, consider:

What will I earn?

What will I learn?

What will this experience qualify me for next?

Can I realistically afford the transport and other costs?

Are the working conditions acceptable?

If the answers are poor across the board, continuing the search may be wiser.

A practical first-year career plan

Your first year after university does not need to produce a perfect career.

A better target is to finish the year with evidence of progress.

Choose one technical or professional skill to deepen and one workplace habit to improve.

For example:

  • Accounting + professional reporting

  • Programming + software development practices

  • Marketing + analytics

  • Teaching + lesson planning

  • Research + data analysis

  • Sales + customer relationship management

  • Administration + Excel and reporting

Keep records of what you accomplish.

Instead of writing "responsible for customer service" on your CV a year later, you might be able to say that you handled customer enquiries, maintained records, supported a specific process or helped improve a measurable result.

That is much stronger evidence.

Final advice for fresh graduates in Uganda

Do not measure your career by how quickly someone else got hired.

Your first job is an entry point, not a verdict on your degree or your future.

Apply broadly, but apply intelligently. Target graduate trainee programmes, entry-level jobs, internships and junior positions related to your skills. Build practical evidence while you search. Use several job sources and verify vacancies through official employer channels.

Most importantly, do not pay someone because they promised to "connect you" to a job.

A strong graduate application does not pretend that you have five years of experience. It makes a convincing case that you have enough relevant knowledge, practical ability and willingness to learn to become useful quickly.

That is a much more realistic advantage to build.
