10 Best National Parks in Uganda to Visit Before You Die



Uganda is unusually good for travellers who want more than a conventional safari. Within the country’s national-park system, you can watch elephants and lions on the savannah, follow chimpanzees through tropical forest, trek towards mountain gorillas, hike volcanic slopes and spend days in remote wilderness.

The tricky part is choosing where to go.

Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) currently manages 10 national parks, ranging from Murchison Falls in the north-west to Mount Elgon in the east and Kidepo Valley in the north-east.

There is no objectively “best” park. The right choice depends on whether you want a classic game drive, primates, gorillas, hiking, birding, scenery, a short trip from Kampala or a proper wilderness adventure.

Here are the 10 parks worth putting on your Uganda travel list.

1. Murchison Falls National Park — for a classic Uganda safari

Murchison Falls is one of Uganda's defining safari destinations.

The park combines broad savannah landscapes with the Victoria Nile and the dramatic Murchison Falls, where the river is forced through a narrow gorge before continuing downstream.

For many visitors, the attraction is the combination of game drives and a Nile boat trip rather than a single activity. The landscape also gives the trip a distinctly different character from Uganda's forest parks.

Murchison Falls is large, so accommodation and activity locations matter when planning your itinerary. Staying on one side of the park can make some activities considerably less convenient than they appear on a map.

Best for: first-time safari travellers, wildlife viewing, river scenery and multi-day trips.

Plan for: at least 2–3 days if you want to combine game drives with other activities.

UWA lists Murchison Falls among Uganda's 10 national parks.

2. Queen Elizabeth National Park — for wildlife variety

Queen Elizabeth National Park is one of the more versatile choices for a Uganda safari.

Its landscapes include savannah, wetlands, lakes and the famous Kazinga Channel, where boat trips provide a different way of seeing wildlife.

The wider park also includes the Ishasha sector, known for its tree-climbing lions. Seeing them is never guaranteed, however, so an itinerary should not be built around a single animal encounter.

Queen Elizabeth works particularly well as part of a western Uganda circuit. It can be combined with destinations such as Kibale, Bwindi or the Rwenzori region depending on how much time you have.

Best for: first safaris, mixed wildlife experiences, boat trips and western Uganda circuits.

Plan for: 2–3 days for a useful introduction, longer if combining several sectors.

3. Bwindi Impenetrable National Park — for mountain gorilla trekking

If your main reason for visiting Uganda is mountain gorillas, Bwindi belongs near the top of your itinerary.

The forest is not a conventional drive-through safari destination. Gorilla trekking involves walking through forest terrain with trained guides and rangers, and the experience can range from relatively manageable to physically demanding depending on the group and conditions.

Bwindi also has several trekking sectors, so where you stay matters. A lodge that looks close to Bwindi on a booking website may not necessarily be close to the trailhead you need.

How much does gorilla trekking cost?

UWA announced a new low-season permit structure in 2026. During April, May and November, gorilla tracking permits are listed at US$600 for foreign non-residents and US$500 for foreign residents. These discounted permits cannot be rescheduled.

UWA has also announced rates taking effect 1 January 2027, including a standard gorilla-tracking rate of US$800 for foreign non-residents, US$700 for foreign residents and UGX300,000 for East African Community visitors.

Those figures cover the permit itself, not your transport, accommodation, meals, tips and other trip costs.

Best for: travellers whose priority is seeing mountain gorillas.

Plan for: advance permit arrangements, suitable accommodation and transport to the correct trekking sector.

4. Kibale National Park — for chimpanzees and primates

Kibale is the park to consider if your idea of wildlife is less “lion on the horizon” and more chimpanzee moving through the trees above your head.

The park is particularly associated with chimpanzee tracking and habituation experiences, alongside its wider collection of forest primates.

That makes Kibale a natural companion to Queen Elizabeth National Park or a western Uganda itinerary.

Chimpanzee trekking is also substantially different from a savannah game drive. You spend much of the experience walking through forest, following signs and listening for animals rather than simply driving from one sighting to another.

UWA's 2026 tariff announcement lists chimpanzee tracking at Kibale at US$300 for foreign non-residents, US$200 for foreign residents and UGX180,000 for East African Community visitors from 1 January 2027. A lower-season rate of US$200 for foreign non-residents and US$150 for foreign residents applies during April, May and November under the announced 2026 arrangement.

Best for: chimpanzees, primates, forest walks and nature enthusiasts.

Plan for: comfortable walking shoes, rain protection and enough time for the forest experience rather than treating it like a quick photo stop.

5. Kidepo Valley National Park — for remote wilderness

Kidepo is the choice for travellers who want Uganda to feel genuinely remote.

Located in north-eastern Uganda, Kidepo offers expansive landscapes and a very different atmosphere from the more frequently visited western parks. The journey itself becomes part of the experience.

That remoteness comes with a price: time and logistics.

You need to think seriously about transport, fuel, vehicle reliability, accommodation and the amount of time available. Trying to squeeze Kidepo into an already crowded itinerary can turn an exciting wilderness trip into a long exercise in watching the clock.

Best for: remote wilderness, scenery, photography and travellers with more time.

Plan for: several days rather than a rushed overnight visit.

Kidepo Valley is one of the 10 national parks officially listed by UWA.

6. Lake Mburo National Park — for a shorter safari

Lake Mburo is one of the more practical choices when you want a wildlife experience without committing to a much longer journey into western or northern Uganda.

The park offers savannah scenery and wildlife experiences around the lake and surrounding landscapes. Its location also makes it useful as part of a trip towards western Uganda.

Lake Mburo can be particularly interesting for travellers looking for activities beyond a conventional vehicle-based game drive. However, rules and availability for activities such as walking or cycling should be confirmed with UWA or your operator before travelling.

Best for: shorter safaris, road trips and travellers heading towards western Uganda.

Plan for: 1–2 nights for a compact visit, or longer if combining it with other destinations.

7. Mgahinga Gorilla National Park — for gorillas, volcanoes and hiking

Mgahinga is small compared with some of Uganda's larger parks, but its setting is anything but ordinary.

The park sits within the Virunga landscape and combines mountain forests, volcanic scenery and gorilla-related experiences.

It is a particularly interesting choice for someone who wants wildlife and hiking in the same trip.

Mountain conditions can change quickly, so proper footwear, rain protection and suitable clothing matter. Trekking should be approached as a physical outdoor activity rather than a leisurely forest stroll.

Best for: gorillas, volcanoes, hiking and dramatic mountain scenery.

Plan for: appropriate fitness, weather protection and a sensible itinerary with recovery time.

8. Rwenzori Mountains National Park — for serious mountain trekking

The Rwenzori Mountains offer one of Uganda's most demanding outdoor experiences.

This is not the park to visit if you simply want to jump out of a vehicle, take a photograph and be back at your hotel by lunch.

Multi-day Rwenzori treks involve altitude, changing weather, difficult terrain and considerable physical preparation. Depending on the route, trekkers need to consider equipment, food, accommodation, porters, guides and emergency arrangements.

The reward is a mountain environment unlike Uganda's savannah parks, including spectacular high-altitude scenery.

Best for: experienced hikers, mountaineers and travellers looking for a serious adventure.

Plan for: multiple days, physical preparation and a reputable guiding arrangement.

UWA lists Rwenzori Mountains among Uganda's national parks.

9. Mount Elgon National Park — for hiking in eastern Uganda

Mount Elgon provides another mountain option, but the experience differs considerably from the Rwenzori.

The park is centred on Mount Elgon in eastern Uganda, with forest, highland environments and extensive hiking opportunities.

It can also fit naturally into a broader eastern Uganda trip, particularly if you are exploring the region around Mbale and nearby attractions.

Weather on the mountain can differ from conditions in lower-lying towns, so don't pack purely according to the forecast you saw before leaving Kampala.

Best for: hiking, highland scenery and eastern Uganda road trips.

Plan for: a suitable guide, proper hiking footwear and weather-resistant clothing.

10. Semuliki National Park — for forest, birds and unusual landscapes

Semuliki is one of Uganda's more unusual national parks.

Located in western Uganda near the Rwenzori Mountains, it protects a lowland tropical forest with strong ecological links to the Congo Basin. UWA describes it as the easternmost extension of the Ituri Forest and highlights its distinctive Central African character.

The park is particularly interesting for birding, forest walks and visitors who want something different from the country's classic savannah safari.

Semuliki also has hot springs and a landscape that feels markedly different from Queen Elizabeth or Murchison Falls.

Best for: birdwatchers, forest enthusiasts and travellers exploring western Uganda.

Plan for: realistic road-time calculations, especially when combining Semuliki with other western destinations.

How much does a Uganda safari cost?

There is no single price for a trip to a Ugandan national park.

Your total budget can include:

  • UWA park-entry fees
  • Gorilla or chimpanzee permits where applicable
  • Accommodation
  • Vehicle hire or transport
  • Fuel
  • Driver-guide fees
  • Boat trips and other activities
  • Food
  • Tips
  • Travel insurance
  • Flights or domestic transfers

This is why two people can visit the same park and spend dramatically different amounts.

Don't use an old blog post as your final price reference. UWA changes tariffs and activity fees, and its official rates page currently carries a 2026–2027 tariff update.

There has already been a significant 2026 change for primate tourism: UWA introduced discounted low-season gorilla and chimpanzee permits for April, May and November and suspended the previous seven-day unpaid permit reservation window from 1 March 2026. Permits must now be secured through payment at booking.

For the latest fees, booking requirements and activity rules, check UWA directly before paying an operator.

When is the best time to visit Uganda's national parks?

There isn't one perfect month for every park.

Your preferred experience matters.

For conventional savannah wildlife viewing, many travellers prefer drier periods because roads and some wildlife-viewing conditions can be easier. Forest trekking is a different proposition, because rain can make trails muddy while also affecting animal behaviour and vegetation.

Interestingly, UWA's new chimpanzee and gorilla low-season permit structure specifically covers April, May and November, making those months worth considering for travellers who are flexible and qualify for the applicable permit category.

Before travelling, check:

  1. Current UWA fees.
  2. Permit availability.
  3. Road conditions.
  4. Your accommodation's location relative to the activity.
  5. Weather conditions for the specific park.
  6. Whether your chosen activity requires a guide or advance booking.

What should you pack for a Uganda safari?

Your packing list depends heavily on the park.

For a general safari, consider:

  • Lightweight, neutral-coloured clothing
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • A hat and sunglasses
  • Sunscreen
  • Insect repellent
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Binoculars
  • Camera and spare batteries/power bank
  • Rain jacket
  • Small daypack
  • Personal medication
  • Copies of important travel documents

For gorilla trekking and mountain hikes, upgrade the footwear and rain protection. Forest and mountain terrain can become muddy, slippery and surprisingly cold.

Responsible wildlife tourism in Uganda

A good wildlife trip is not simply about getting the perfect photograph.

Follow ranger and guide instructions, maintain the required distance from animals, don't feed wildlife, don't leave rubbish behind and don't attempt to touch or provoke animals.

UWA's conservation work includes law enforcement against illegal activities such as poaching and the removal of snares and traps by rangers.

Your spending also forms part of the wider tourism economy around protected areas. Choosing legitimate accommodation, guides and tour operators helps keep more tourism activity within the formal conservation and local tourism system.

Most importantly, don't treat wildlife as a theme park attraction. Animals do not owe visitors a photograph.

Are these really the only national parks in Uganda?

For the current national-park system, yes: UWA lists 10 national parks. They are Kidepo Valley, Bwindi Impenetrable, Kibale, Lake Mburo, Semuliki, Queen Elizabeth, Murchison Falls, Rwenzori Mountains, Mount Elgon and Mgahinga Gorilla National Parks.

However, Uganda's protected-area system is changing. UWA's 2026 State of Wildlife Resources report discusses proposed upgrades involving several wildlife reserves and forest areas. Those proposals should not be confused with the current list of officially established national parks until the relevant approvals are completed.

The bottom line

Uganda doesn't have one type of wildlife holiday.

You can spend your trip looking for lions and elephants on the savannah, follow chimpanzees through Kibale's forest, trek towards gorillas in Bwindi or Mgahinga, hike the Rwenzori or Mount Elgon, or disappear into the remote landscapes of Kidepo.

The smartest way to choose is simple:

Decide what you want to experience first, then choose the park that provides it.

And before handing over your money, check the latest UWA tariff, permit availability, access arrangements and activity requirements. Wildlife tourism is one area where an old price list can become a rather expensive souvenir.
