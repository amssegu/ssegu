MTN MoMo vs Airtel Money: Which Is Cheaper in Uganda?

Compare MTN MoMo and Airtel Money fees in Uganda for sending money, cash withdrawals and bill payments, with practical tips

SSEGUPost2026-10-31



Mobile money has become part of the ordinary arithmetic of life in Uganda. A worker sends money home after payday, a tenant pays rent from a phone, a shopkeeper settles a supplier's bill, and someone else withdraws a few thousand shillings to cover transport and lunch. Each transaction is convenient, but convenience comes at a price.

For customers choosing between MTN Mobile Money (MoMo) and Airtel Money, the difference is not always as straightforward as competing advertisements might suggest. The cost depends on what you are doing, how much you are moving and whether you intend to keep the money in your wallet or turn it into cash.


A comparison of the operators' published tariffs reveals that several common transactions attract identical charges. The more useful distinction is between sending money, withdrawing it and paying bills, because each follows its own fee structure.


Sending money: the difference may be smaller than you expect

Consider someone sending UGX 10,000 to a friend. On the published tariff schedules reviewed for this comparison, the sending charge is UGX 500 on both MTN MoMo and Airtel Money.

The same published fee applies to a UGX 50,000 transfer, while sending UGX 100,000 costs UGX 1,000 on either service. At these amounts, switching networks solely to save on transfer charges offers little advantage.

The amount being transferred matters as much as the headline fee. A UGX 500 charge on UGX 10,000 represents 5 per cent of the amount sent. The same charge on UGX 50,000 represents 1 per cent. The fee has not changed, but its impact on the sender's money has.


There is also the question of who receives the money. MTN and Airtel customers regularly transact across networks, so choosing a wallet is not necessarily a matter of sending money exclusively to people on the same network. Check the relevant tariff for the transfer you intend to make, particularly when sending across networks or using a service with a different fee structure.

The final confirmation screen on your phone is the most useful check before entering your PIN. An old tariff table, even one published by a reputable website, is no substitute for the charge currently being applied to your transaction.


Withdrawing cash is where the arithmetic changes

Receiving money on a phone is one thing; getting physical cash into your hands is another. For Ugandans who depend on mobile money for everyday spending, withdrawal charges can be just as important as sending fees.

Both operators publish separate agent-withdrawal charges and applicable withdrawal-tax ranges. The published schedules reviewed here show the following costs for selected amounts.

A UGX 10,000 withdrawal, for example, can attract an additional UGX 725–775 in the figures above. The agent fee alone does not tell the full story.


For someone withdrawing small amounts repeatedly, the cumulative cost can become noticeable. Withdrawing UGX 10,000 several times a week means paying the applicable charge each time. Where practical, planning withdrawals around actual spending needs can reduce unnecessary transactions, although carrying large amounts of cash has its own risks.

The choice of agent matters, too. An agent who is nearby, has enough cash and completes transactions reliably may be more useful than one offering a marginally better rate but requiring an expensive trip across town. A published tariff is only one part of the cost of accessing your money.


Paying a bill is not the same as sending money

Mobile money has expanded beyond person-to-person transfers. Ugandans use it to pay for electricity, water, television subscriptions, school fees and other services. Yet the fee for paying a bill can differ from the charge for sending the same amount to another person.

MTN's tariff schedule distinguishes selected payments, including school fees and certain payment services, from utility and other service categories. Airtel also publishes separate categories for utility payments and other transactions.


The following examples illustrate the difference between published payment categories for a UGX 10,000 transaction.

For a household paying recurring utility bills, these distinctions are worth understanding. The cost of a transaction depends on the service selected, not simply on whether the customer uses MTN or Airtel.


It is also unwise to assume that a fee listed under ordinary payments applies to bank transfers, merchant payments or every other service available through a mobile-money menu. Those transactions may have their own charges. Enter the correct biller and amount, then inspect the displayed fee before confirming payment.


So, which wallet should Ugandans choose?

The published figures do not establish a universal winner. For the common sending amounts and selected withdrawal and bill-payment categories compared above, the charges are similar. The decision therefore rests partly on how the wallet fits into everyday life.

Someone who regularly sends money to relatives on one network may prefer the wallet that makes those transfers straightforward. A market trader might care more about dependable access to cash and the ability to pay suppliers. A household paying electricity and water bills should compare the relevant payment categories rather than relying on the advertised cost of sending money.


It is worth remembering that a cheaper transaction is not necessarily the cheapest overall arrangement. Transport to an agent, an unsuccessful withdrawal, delays and the inconvenience of maintaining two wallets can all affect the practical value of a service. Keeping both SIM cards active may make sense for someone who regularly deals with customers on different networks, but the benefit should justify the extra complexity.

Before making a large transfer or paying an unfamiliar bill, consult the official tariff schedules:

  • MTN Uganda: Mobile Money Tariffs

  • Airtel Money Uganda: Transaction Fees

If a tariff page does not load properly, check the relevant mobile-money menu or ask an authorised agent for the current charges. Do not rely on an older online comparison when the transaction screen shows a different amount.


Ultimately, the better wallet is the one that serves your usual transactions reliably at a competitive cost. For many Ugandans, the difference between MTN MoMo and Airtel Money will not be decided by a single transfer fee. It will emerge from the small, repeated costs of moving, withdrawing and spending money throughout the month.
