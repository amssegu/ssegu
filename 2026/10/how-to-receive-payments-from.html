How to Receive Payments from International Clients in Uganda

Learn how to receive payments from international clients in Uganda using bank transfers, Upwork, Fiverr and Payoneer. Compare fees

SSEGUPost2026-10-22


Winning an international client is a milestone for any Ugandan freelancer. A web developer in Kampala might land a website redesign for a British business. A writer might secure a regular assignment from an American publication. Someone working remotely might finally have a client willing to pay in dollars rather than shillings, and for any Ugandan freelancer, you know how valuable such a client would be.

But then once the work is done, one ought to wonder; how does the money actually reach Uganda?

It is easy to underestimate this part of working online. A client may suggest PayPal because it is familiar to them, while a freelancer may prefer a bank transfer or a payment service such as Payoneer. The arrangement can sound straightforward until someone discovers that a particular withdrawal method is unavailable, a transfer attracts unexpected charges or the money arrives in an account at a less favourable exchange rate.

For freelancers, writers, designers, software developers and other professionals earning abroad, choosing a payment method is part of running the business. The amount agreed with a client matters, but so does the amount that eventually reaches your account.

The payment method matters before the project begins

Consider a Ugandan developer who agrees to build a website for US$300. The client wants to pay through a service they already use, and the developer agrees without checking whether the service supports receiving and withdrawing commercial payments in Uganda.

The work is completed, but the payment arrangement turns out to be more complicated than expected. Perhaps the account can be used to make purchases but not to receive the particular payment. Perhaps the money can be received but not withdrawn through the method the developer anticipated.

The problem is no longer simply how to get paid. It is how to recover money already earned.

That is why payment arrangements should be settled before work starts. Both parties should agree on the currency, payment schedule, fees and receiving method. A freelancer should also verify that the chosen route works for their own account, rather than relying on another person's experience or a recommendation from an online discussion.

For someone working through Upwork or Fiverr, the first place to look is the platform's own withdrawal settings. Direct clients offer more flexibility, but that also means the freelancer must take greater responsibility for arranging payment securely.

Bank transfers remain worth considering

A direct international bank transfer can be a sensible option when working with an established overseas company or a client paying a substantial invoice. It avoids the need to build an arrangement around a freelance marketplace, although it is not necessarily the cheapest method.

Ugandan banks can provide information about receiving international transfers, including the account details required, supported currencies, applicable charges and any intermediary-bank arrangements. The exact requirements depend on the bank and the transaction.

Before accepting a transfer, ask your bank for its official incoming international-payment instructions. You may need your account name, account number, bank details and SWIFT/BIC code, where applicable. Do not assume that every account can receive every currency or that the full amount sent will arrive without deductions.

Currency conversion deserves particular attention. A client might send US dollars, but the amount ultimately credited to your account in Ugandan shillings depends on the conversion arrangements and any applicable charges. Ask whether the funds will be converted automatically, what exchange rate will apply and whether you can receive or retain the original currency.

Bank transfers are often easier to justify for larger invoices than for very small payments, particularly when fixed charges apply. For a US$20 assignment, a combination of transfer and intermediary fees could take an unreasonable share of the earnings. For a recurring contract worth several hundred dollars, the same arrangement may be more practical.

The answer depends on the actual charges, not simply on whether a bank advertises international transfers.

Freelance platforms have their own payment systems

For people finding work through online marketplaces, using an established withdrawal route can simplify the process.

Upwork documents several payment methods, including Direct to Local Bank in supported countries and third-party services such as Payoneer and PayPal. The options available depend on location and account eligibility. Upwork lists a US$0.99 charge for Direct to Local Bank withdrawals where that method is available, but Ugandan freelancers should confirm that it appears in their own accounts and check for any additional bank charges.

Its official guide, How to Get Paid, explains the available arrangements and their conditions.

Fiverr also provides withdrawal options that can include Payoneer-linked transfers and PayPal, subject to eligibility. Its official Payoneer payout guide explains how the process works.

The important distinction is between a payment method supported by a platform and one available to a particular seller. An option appearing in a tutorial or on another freelancer's screen does not establish that it will be available to every Ugandan account.

There is also a difference between paying for a service and withdrawing money earned by providing one. The two transactions may follow different rules, even when they involve the same provider.

Payoneer and PayPal require different kinds of caution

Payoneer is worth investigating for freelancers and businesses receiving money from international clients or supported marketplaces. Depending on eligibility and the type of transaction, it can provide ways to receive payments and withdraw funds to a linked bank account.

Its usefulness depends on the particular service required. Receiving a payment from another Payoneer customer, collecting eligible marketplace earnings and using payment-receiving account details are not necessarily identical arrangements. Fees can also vary according to the transaction, currency and account.

Before registering, review Payoneer's pricing and its information on withdrawing funds. Check the availability of the specific features you intend to use as a Ugandan resident.

PayPal calls for equally careful verification. Its international recognition can create the impression that anyone can receive money and withdraw it locally in the same way. In practice, account capabilities vary by country.

The ability to open an account, make online purchases, receive commercial payments and withdraw funds should not be treated as interchangeable. Check PayPal's country information and applicable supported features before agreeing to accept payment through it.

If the required service is unavailable, negotiate an alternative with the client. Using false registration details or another person's account to bypass restrictions can create verification problems and put the money at risk.

The money agreed is not always the money received

Payment fees can quietly undermine a freelancer's earnings, particularly on small projects.

Suppose a client agrees to pay US$100. The platform deducts a commission, the payment service charges a withdrawal fee and the exchange rate offered is less favourable than the rate the freelancer used when pricing the job. The final amount in shillings may be noticeably lower than expected.

There is no universal deduction that applies to every international payment. The total depends on the route and the charges involved.

A freelancer should therefore compare the net amount received, rather than looking at a single advertised fee. A service with a low withdrawal charge may still be expensive if its currency-conversion margin is high. Conversely, a bank transfer with several possible charges might remain competitive for a sufficiently large payment.

The frequency of withdrawals matters, too. If a payment service applies a fixed charge each time money is withdrawn, transferring small amounts repeatedly may cost more than consolidating them. That does not mean freelancers should leave money in an account indefinitely; they should balance fees against cash-flow needs and the risks of keeping funds with a third party.

For regular clients, the simplest solution is often to agree on a payment schedule that suits both sides. Weekly, fortnightly or monthly invoices may be more practical than arranging a separate transfer for every small task.

Getting paid safely requires more than choosing a provider

Payment disputes often begin with unclear expectations. A freelancer completes work without a deposit, sends the finished files and discovers that the client disputes the price or disappears. A payment screenshot may look convincing, but it is not proof that cleared funds are available.

For larger projects, written contracts and milestone payments can reduce exposure. Freelancers using marketplaces should follow the platform's payment-protection rules. Those working directly with clients should document the scope of work, price, deadlines, revisions and payment terms before starting.

Fraud deserves particular attention. A supposed client may send an excess payment and request a refund, claim that a fee must be paid to release earnings, or send a message pretending to come from a payment provider. Never refund an unverified payment or share passwords, PINs and one-time verification codes. Check transactions through the official account or bank rather than trusting screenshots and emails.

Freelancers earning regularly should also keep invoices, contracts, payment confirmations and records of exchange rates and fees. Receiving money from abroad does not automatically make the income exempt from Ugandan tax obligations. Anyone uncertain about reporting requirements or business registration can consult the Uganda Revenue Authority or a qualified tax professional. The Bank of Uganda is a useful starting point for information about regulated financial services and consumer protection.

Make the payment arrangement part of the business

There is no single best way for every Ugandan freelancer to receive international payments. Someone working through Upwork may find that the platform's supported withdrawal options are sufficient. A developer dealing directly with an overseas company may prefer a bank transfer. Another professional may find Payoneer useful for eligible transactions.

What matters is confirming the arrangement before the work begins, understanding the full cost and ensuring that the money can actually be withdrawn in Uganda.

International clients can expand the opportunities available to Ugandan professionals, but earning in a foreign currency is only valuable if the payment process works. A reliable arrangement, clear terms and sensible records are not administrative details to sort out later. They are part of getting paid for the work you have already done.

Payment-provider fees, eligibility requirements and withdrawal options can change. Confirm current terms directly with your bank or provider before accepting a payment.
